## Problem-oriented synthesis

The primary work falls into four questions that must remain separate:

1. **Which unlabeled update objective is sensible?** Entropy (Tent/EATA/SAR), marginal view entropy (MEMO/TPT), contrastive/source reward (CLIPTTA/RLCF), calibration-aware or Tsallis objectives (2026 papers), and cache/prototype mechanisms (TDA/DPE/BCA/WATT) each target a different mechanism. Success at adapting a model does not show that objective changes rank alternative Ramen supports.
2. **Can we detect reliability or adaptation harm?** EATA, DeYO, SoTTA, AETTA and the 2026 selective-adaptation paper study sample/stream screening, selective adapt/skip, or estimated performance. CAS is an established per-sample multi-view baseline. Their action granularity and deployment setting differ from Ramen's within-query support swaps; labels and OOD fields stay evaluator-only here.
3. **Can unlabeled evidence choose an action?** MORPHEUS predicts among whole TTA procedures from unlabeled test features, but its regressor is meta-trained on labeled post-adaptation accuracies. Cygert et al. study hyperparameter/method selection with varied information budgets. Jhawar–Wang explicitly frame action identifiability across deployment worlds and establish a direct conceptual prior. These rule out generic novelty framing.
4. **Can action utility be attributed to a support identity?** TracIn, GRAD-MATCH and GLISTER explain gradient/data valuation when a labeled validation objective supplies the target. The unresolved local question is whether a label-free observation, rather than that evaluator target, can order exact support substitutions within one frozen Ramen state.

The candidate contribution, if it survives further source/code comparison, is therefore a bounded **query-local support-action audit**, positioned as a diagnostic adjacent to action-selection, selective-adaptation and data-valuation work. It should report exact objective-to-utility relationships and the compute cost of that information. It must not claim a new general theory of unlabeled action identifiability, a new selective-adaptation problem, a universal selector, or impossibility from finite empirical failures.

## P1 gap decision

The high-level G1 claim is dropped as a novelty claim. Continue to protocol design because there is a narrower experiment to decide: whether objective changes after exact same-query, same-cache, one-support-swap updates predict that query's evaluator-only CE change under a fixed Ramen update. The contrastive, calibration and cache families give candidate observations, not proof of signal. The scientific contribution is provisional and may amount to a negative diagnostic/extension rather than a new method.

Do not describe the local study as discovering action identifiability, proving finite-sample information limits, or demonstrating a deployable label-free selector. If later sources or raw comparisons erase the action-granularity distinction, pivot or stop before claiming novelty.
